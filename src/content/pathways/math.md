Many research programmes around distributed systems, category-theoretic composition, verifiable logs, anonymous credentials, and CRDT convergence remain separate from community software. Neighbourhoods brings those questions into one applied framework.

The goal is not decorative formalism. The goal is software whose correctness properties matter because real groups will use it to coordinate.

## The formal surface

Streaming x-machines provide a way to treat stateful stream processors as composable machines. Grammars define records, entities, state machines, verbs, and presentations; emitters produce TypeScript and Rust runtimes with canonical wire-byte parity.

This gives researchers an implementation site where algebraic composition, type safety, deterministic transducers, and distributed convergence have practical consequences.

## Cryptographic questions

The identity roadmap includes device-attestation graphs, social recovery, anonymous credentials, unlinkable attribution, epoch-scoped peer identifiers, privacy floors, and tamper-evident histories. These are not isolated primitives; they are tied to the problem of group coordination under different privacy and accountability regimes.

## Why help

Neighbourhoods needs scrutiny. The most useful contributions may be proofs, counterexamples, protocol review, encoding review, privacy analysis, formal models of grammar composition, or better explanations that make the underlying mathematics legible to builders.
