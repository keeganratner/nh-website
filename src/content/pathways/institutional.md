Consumer collaboration products optimise for engagement. Enterprise products optimise for seat growth and procurement. Neither category is shaped around the constraints carried by public institutions, cooperative banks, civic consortia, universities, foundations, and multi-stakeholder programmes.

Neighbourhoods offers a formal coordination framework for institutions that need shared records, role-sensitive permissions, verifiable state, and durable interoperability.

## The constraint shape

Institutional coordination often requires data sovereignty, audit trails, regulated access, long-lived records, multi-party accountability, and the ability to outlast vendors. These are not add-ons to a collaboration app; they belong in the foundation.

Neighbourhoods separates identity, spaces, grammars, runtime, replication, logs, and settlement so each layer can be inspected and governed according to its own risk profile.

## Multi-stakeholder coordination

A regional programme may involve local communities, councils, funders, service providers, technical partners, and public agencies. Each actor has its own obligations and internal systems. A shared grammar can define the collaboration layer without absorbing every participant into one institution.

This makes Neighbourhoods suitable for consortia where the coordination problem is real, the governance surface is sensitive, and platform dependency is unacceptable.

## Adoption path

Federated deployment provides a practical starting point. Spaces can run with relays and servers where useful, while the framework continues toward deeper peer-to-peer sovereignty. The point is not serverlessness as ideology; the point is that deployment infrastructure should not become the principal in the trust model.
