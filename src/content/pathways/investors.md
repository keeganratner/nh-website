For the last two decades, digital economies have been organised around applications and platforms. The app defined the grammar of participation: marketplace, feed, task board, DAO vote, chat server, CRM, payments flow.

Neighbourhoods moves the load-bearing layer below the app. Communities define executable grammars; interfaces can then be generated, adapted, or replaced without losing the underlying coordination logic.

## From apps to executable economic patterns

A marketplace app defines how buyers and sellers interact. A governance app defines how proposals and votes work. A reputation platform defines what counts as trust. In each case, the platform owns the grammar.

Neighbourhoods reverses that relation. The community owns the grammar, and the interface is generated around it. That makes coordination patterns reusable assets rather than one-off application code.

## The wedge

The immediate wedge is grammar-based groupware for communities, cooperatives, civic networks, institutional consortia, and distributed organisations that cannot fit their rules into fixed SaaS products. The longer-term market is a grammar marketplace: reusable coordination systems that can be commissioned, licensed, forked, adapted, and funded across many communities.

A soil-regeneration grammar, a local-lending grammar, a care-network grammar, a cooperative-governance grammar, or a civic-budgeting grammar can become a repeatable economic object.

## Why this is investable

The next wave of software will not simply be more apps. AI lowers the cost of interface generation; it does not solve trust, state validity, contribution accounting, identity, or settlement. Neighbourhoods is positioned where those questions converge.

The framework creates economies of coordination: developers build once for a pattern, communities adapt the pattern locally, funders support reusable infrastructure, and reputation can move across contexts.
