Your group already has a way of working. People know who verifies a contribution, who can invite a new member, how a decision becomes real, when a project is ready, and what counts as care, labour, trust, or stewardship.

Most platforms ask communities to squeeze that knowledge into someone else’s product model. Neighbourhoods begins from the other direction: describe your coordination pattern, then let the software form around it.

## The bargain we are trying to dissolve

Convenient platforms often hold your relationships, your records, and your member list. Sovereign alternatives often ask your community to absorb the cost of running a small technical operations team. Neighbourhoods is designed as a third path: shared infrastructure for groups that want practical usability without surrendering agency.

A neighbourhood is not only a place. It is a group of people with shared purpose, shared context, and shared responsibility. Neighbourhoods gives that group a way to make its coordination explicit.

## What a grammar gives your group

A grammar defines the valid moves in your community system: what exists, what members can do, what states things move through, and what conditions must be satisfied before something changes.

That means a soil batch cannot be approved before validation, a mutual-aid request cannot be closed before fulfilment, a cooperative proposal cannot be enacted before the agreed process has completed, and a contribution can be recognised with context rather than flattened into a generic score.

## What you can build

A local food network can coordinate growers, kitchens, transport, payments, and surplus. A repair circle can track tools, events, parts, and skilled volunteers. A care network can protect sensitive information while coordinating meals, transport, respite, and reimbursements. A cooperative can run proposals, objections, budgets, working groups, and delegated roles without forcing its governance into a generic voting tool.

The interface can be simple. The underlying structure can remain yours.
