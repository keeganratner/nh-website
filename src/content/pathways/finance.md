Modern finance recognises wages, invoices, credit scores, collateral, assets, equity, debt, and formal credentials. Much of what keeps communities productive does not fit those categories: stewardship, repair, mediation, care, coordination, local knowledge, trust, and contribution to common infrastructure.

Neighbourhoods gives communities a way to record those contributions inside grammars that define what happened, how it was validated, and how another community may interpret it.

## From abstract trust to situated reputation

Reputation should not collapse into a universal score. A person may be trusted as a compost steward, repair organiser, cooperative treasurer, peer reviewer, childcare coordinator, or mediator. Each form of trust is meaningful because it is attached to a grammar: the actions, states, validations, and attestations that define what was actually done.

That makes reputation portable without making it generic. A verified contribution in one neighbourhood can become economically meaningful in another, if both communities understand the grammar or a compatible mapping.

## Changing the economy of scale

Industrial economies scale by centralising production, logistics, capital, and data. That reduces some costs, but it also creates brittle dependencies. Neighbourhoods enables a different scale logic: shared grammars reduce coordination costs while production, governance, and identity remain locally held.

A local finance circle, a repair cooperative, a food network, and a soil project can each remain autonomous while reusing compatible patterns for contribution, verification, fulfilment, reputation, and value flow.

## Finance for local capacity

AI, local production, local energy, modular manufacturing, and community finance all lower the minimum viable scale for economic activity. But local economies still need accounting, verification, governance, and settlement. Neighbourhoods provides the coordination layer that can make local capacity legible enough to fund, insure, procure from, or trade with.

The deeper shift is from platform economies of scale to protocol economies of coordination.
